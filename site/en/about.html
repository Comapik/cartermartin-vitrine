The duo

Linaupe Carter & Allan Martin

Two photographers, one single image. They work in Normandy and never sign separately.

Linaupe Carter comes from theatre and stage lighting; Allan Martin from nocturnal documentary photography. Their meeting, in 2026, produced a simple method: build the scene around a real flame, and keep the exposure long enough for the place to breathe.

“Let there be more light” was made that same year: ten scenes shot between an inn, a private library, a vaulted cellar, two nights in the countryside and the studio where the still lifes are built. The prints are made there by hand, one at a time, then mounted on stretchers.

Linaupe Carter

Light & set

Trained in stage lighting, she builds every set by hand — wax, smoke, cloth, stone — and decides where the flame will stand.

Allan Martin

Frame & exposure

A night documentary photographer, he frames the image, times the exposure and oversees the mounting of every work.

“The flame decides everything: the colour, the frame, how long we have before it dies down.”
